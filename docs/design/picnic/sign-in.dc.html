One page, two steps, at /sign-in. Built from Page, Field, Input, Button and Banner as they are: nothing new in Dino. The heading is the screen title (Display XL), with no squiggle: this is a form to get through, so the fun stays on the pages behind it.

A 400 px column. The heading, the text and the form sit in one column at most 400 px wide, on the page’s left edge like every other page, so the field never stretches across a laptop and the text stays under 50 characters a line.

One peach button per step. Send code, then Sign in. Use a different email is secondary, beside Sign in where it fits and under it where it doesn’t (it never fits at 390 px, because Sign in keeps room for “Signing in…”).

Focus follows the step. When step 2 opens, focus moves to its heading (tabindex −1), so a screen reader reads “Check your email” and the address the code went to; one Tab reaches the Code field. “Use a different email” puts focus back in the email field, the address kept.

The bar on /sign-in shows neither Sign in nor the avatar, as the spec says, so the page never offers itself. Search stays: it goes to the recipes, and the way back is in the address.

Signed in with no name yet, the avatar is Dino’s UserRound icon and the button is named “Account”; its menu has no heading and one item, Sign out. TAS-103 fills in the name, the letter and the heading.

The page shows step 1 at once. While the session is still being checked the form is already there; if it turns out you’re signed in, the page replaces itself with where you were going. No spinner for a check that’s nearly always “signed out”.

Document title: “Sign in · Tastebook” on both steps, like “Page not found · Tastebook”.
